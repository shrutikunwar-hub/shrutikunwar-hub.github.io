01 / Problem Statement

The Question

Mahyco needed to understand the hybrid paddy landscape across four Haryana districts — a region with highly variable adoption and entrenched market leaders. The brief: quantify opportunity, map competition, and deliver a go-to-market recommendation actionable within a single growing season.

The core question: Which districts represent the real entry opportunity — and what does it take to win farmers' and dealers' trust in each?

02 / Methodology

The Approach

Three-stage research design combining structured primary fieldwork across all four districts with secondary data triangulation from government agriculture records.

01

Farmer Surveys

60 farmer interviews per district — stratified by land-holding size (marginal, small, medium, large) with random village selection within each stratum.

02

Dealer Surveys

15 dealer interviews per district — clustered by geography across key retail hubs. Captured margins, brand relationships, and recommendation patterns.

03

Secondary Research

Triangulated with Kisan Bhawan Panchkula records, Haryana Agriculture Department data, and Indiastat / Agristat national databases.

04 / Key Insight

Where the Opportunity Was

The Finding

Ambala was nearly saturated at 64.5% hybridization — the obvious target. Kaithal, the district with the largest paddy acreage at 166,300 hectares, sat at just 7.9%.

The biggest opportunity wasn't where the market was loudest. It was where the market hadn't started yet.

High yield drove 63–81% of adoption decisions across all four districts. Lodging was the #1 barrier everywhere. Company-driven initiatives drove 62–70% of farmers' shift to hybrids — making owned channel strategy more important than advertising.

05 / Key Findings

What the Data Revealed

Yield Is the Primary Purchase Driver

63–81% across districts

Across all four districts, yield performance was the dominant factor in a farmer's decision to switch to hybrid paddy. Messages leading with yield outperform those leading with price or brand heritage.

Lodging Is the Universal Barrier

Top barrier · All 4 districts

Lodging — stem weakness causing plants to fall — was cited as the #1 reason farmers hesitated to adopt hybrids. Varieties with demonstrated lodging resistance should anchor the product strategy.

Market Leaders Hold Structural Moats

60%+ share in each district

Existing players dominated with more than 60% share in every district, largely through entrenched dealer relationships and farmer trust built over multiple seasons. Entry requires a channel-first, demonstration-led approach.

Company Demos Drive Conversion

62–70% of hybrid adopters

The majority of farmers who switched to hybrid paddy cited company-organized field demonstrations and farmer influencer networks as the conversion trigger — not advertising.

06 / Recommendations

What to Do About It

District-specific recommendations built around the 4P framework — tailored to each market's stage of hybrid adoption.

R.01

Lead with Kaithal

Allocate primary market development resources to Kaithal — 166,300 hectares at 7.9% hybridization offers the largest greenfield opportunity with fewest entrenched competitors.

R.02

Demonstration-First Strategy

Invest in field days, crop demonstrations, and farmer influencer networks. 62–70% of hybrid adopters cited company demos as their conversion trigger — not advertising.

R.03

Lead with Lodging Resistance

Position Mahyco's hybrid varieties around lodging resistance and yield performance — the two universal concerns across every farmer segment and district studied.

R.04

Dealer Partnership in Ambala

In near-saturated Ambala, dealer margin incentives and partnership — rather than farmer-facing campaigns — are the most efficient path to incremental share gain.
